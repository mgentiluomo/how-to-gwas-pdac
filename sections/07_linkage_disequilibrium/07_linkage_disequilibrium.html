Linkage disequilibrium and what it decides for you

Why the resolution of a fine-mapping analysis is set by the population, not by the method

Every association a GWAS reports is an association with a region, not with a variant. The scan tests markers, and a marker is informative only because it is correlated with whatever is actually causal nearby. That correlation is linkage disequilibrium, and it is the reason the method works at all.

It is also the reason the method stops working at a certain point. LD is what lets a genotyping array of 400,000 markers stand in for three billion bases; it is equally what prevents the resulting signal from being narrowed to one variant. The same property does both, and no analysis can have the first without the second.

This page is about the second half of that trade, because it is the half that tutorials skip. It concerns how the guide’s fine-mapping result should be read, and it makes a case for ancestral diversity in genetic studies that rests on resolution rather than on fairness.

01 - LD is a property of a population, not of the genome

Why. There is no such thing as the linkage disequilibrium between two variants. There is the LD between them in a particular population, and it can differ severalfold between populations at the same pair of sites.

The reason is demographic history. LD is created by drift and destroyed by recombination, so the balance between them depends on effective population size. A population that has been small has more LD, because its members are closer relatives and fewer recombination events separate them; a population that has been large has less, because recombination has had more independent opportunities to break associations apart doi:10.1534/genetics.114.166454. Bottlenecks and expansions leave distinct and long-lasting signatures on the whole LD curve, and those signatures persist for hundreds of generations after the demographic event that caused them doi:10.1534/genetics.114.166454.

For human populations, one consequence dominates everything else. African populations have the largest ancestral effective size and did not pass through the out-of-Africa bottleneck; non-African populations did. LD therefore extends further in Europe and East Asia than in Africa, and this has been documented at genome scale since the first surveys PMID:11346797 PMID:11967554.

02 - Measuring it on the demonstration data

Why. The statement above is standard and you should not take it on trust when you can check it in the dataset you are already using.

bash scripts/07_linkage_disequilibrium/01_ld_decay.sh

The script computes pairwise r² among common variants across the ABO region, separately within each ancestry group, and averages by physical distance.

Distance between variants European African East Asian
0-10 kb 0.272 0.184 0.344
10-25 kb 0.122 0.063 0.173
25-50 kb 0.047 0.022 0.089
50-100 kb 0.031 0.010 0.029
100-200 kb 0.018 0.006 0.013

Read the African column against the other two. At 25 to 50 kb, average r² in the African group is less than half the European value and about a quarter of the East Asian one. The ordering is the same at every distance, and it is the ordering that population history predicts.

NoteWhat this is not

This is 288 African individuals at one locus on one array, not a genome-wide estimate of LD decay. The magnitudes here should not be quoted as such. What the comparison establishes is the direction and the size of the difference between groups measured in the same way on the same variants, which is the only thing the next section needs.

03 - The consequence: resolution is set before you choose a method

Why. Fine mapping asks which variant in a region is causal. It can only distinguish variants that the data distinguish. Two variants in perfect LD carry identical information about every phenotype, so no amount of statistical sophistication, and no increase in sample size, will ever separate them.

The demonstration dataset makes this concrete. Counting variants in strong LD (r² > 0.8) with the causal variant, within each group:

European African East Asian
Variants indistinguishable from the causal one 11 1 11

This single table explains the fine-mapping result of Fine mapping before any Bayes factor is computed. The European credible set contains eleven variants because Europe contains eleven variants that behave identically at this locus. The credible set is not eleven wide because the method is weak; it is eleven wide because the population is.

In the African group the same locus resolves to a single variant. The causal variant is separable there and is not separable in Europe, and that difference was fixed tens of thousands of years before anyone collected a sample.

ImportantA small credible set is not a resolved locus

The two statements sound alike and are not. A credible set of eleven variants spanning 12 kb looks tidy, and a reader may take the tidiness as success. It is not: it is a compact haplotype block, and the set is small because the region is small, not because the analysis resolved anything within it. In the African group the same locus resolves to one variant, and that is resolution. Report the span, the number of variants, and the local LD alongside the posterior probabilities, so that a reader can tell a resolved locus from a region where resolution was never available doi:10.1038/nrg1123.

04 - Three practical consequences

The LD reference must match the ancestry of the analysis. Summary-statistic fine-mapping and conditional analysis both need an LD matrix, and if that matrix comes from a population with different LD than the study sample, the method is being told correlations that do not hold in the data. This produces credible sets that are confidently wrong rather than honestly wide. Where the study is multi-ancestry, one reference cannot serve, and per-stratum LD is required.

Diversity buys resolution, not only equity. The argument for including non-European participants in genetic studies is usually made on grounds of fairness, and that argument is sufficient on its own. But it is not the only one. The table above shows a locus that is unresolvable in Europe and resolvable in Africa, in the same data, with the same method. Shorter LD is more information per variant. A study that recruits only Europeans is not merely leaving other populations unserved; it is discarding the resolution that would have answered its own question PMID:11037333.

Trans-ancestry analysis helps when the strata are large enough to be heard. In this demonstration, combining the three groups moves the causal variant to first place in the credible set but barely shrinks the set, because the African group that could separate the variants contributes 288 people against 608 Europeans. The mechanism is real and the sample is too small to exploit it. That is the honest reading, and it is also the argument for building consortia in which non-European strata are large enough to change the answer rather than large enough to be mentioned.

Key decisions on this page

  • Whether the LD reference panel matches the ancestry of each analysed stratum, and what was done where it could not.
  • Whether reported credible sets are accompanied by their span and by the local LD, so that width can be attributed to the population rather than to the method.
  • Whether the study design treats ancestral diversity as a source of resolution at the analysis stage, or only as a recruitment target.
